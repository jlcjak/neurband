# neurband — design notes (iteration 1)

A minimal, first-principles surface-EMG wristband like Meta's: the sEMG-RD research
device from *"A generic non-invasive neuromotor interface for human-computer interaction"*
(Nature 2025) and the consumer Meta Neural Band. The aim of this first spin is to **take
real 16-channel EMG data** with as few parts as possible. Polish comes later.

Background research: [research/meta_semg_research.md](research/meta_semg_research.md).

## 1. What we copy and what we simplify

| | Meta sEMG-RD | neurband v1 | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Channels | 16 bipolar | 16 bipolar | lets us use Meta's open datasets/models as a reference |
| Electrodes | 48 gold pogo pins, 6.5 mm, 3 per pod | 48 gold pads 6.5 mm on a flex PCB, 3 per pod (P, bias, N) | flex PCB = no machining; domes/pogos can be soldered on later |
| Pair spacing | 20 mm along the forearm | 20 mm | same geometry |
| Pitch around wrist | 10.6–15 mm, 4 sizes | 4 flex variants: S 8.6 / M 9.5 / L 10.8 / XL 12.4 mm, 7 mm pods | flex cannot stretch; pitches from the fit check |
| Front end | per-pod amplifier, gain 190, 20–850 Hz, 12-bit ADC | central 2× TI ADS1298 (24-bit Σ-Δ, PGA, bias drive, lead-off built in) | one IC per 8 channels replaces ~10 parts per channel |
| Noise | 2.46 µVrms | ≈1.5 µVrms (datasheet, G=6, 4 kSPS, 1 kHz BW) | |
| Sampling | 2 kHz | 4 kSPS (2 kSPS selectable) | ADS1298 −3 dB BW at 2 kSPS is 524 Hz; 4 kSPS keeps 850 Hz |
| Ground/bias | 16 "shield or ground" pins (not described) | middle pin of every pod driven by the ADS1298 right-leg amplifier | big total contact area, rejects mains |
| Radio | BLE | ESP32-S3: Wi-Fi UDP (USB-CDC on the bench via pads) | 1.5 Mbit/s raw is easy on Wi-Fi, hard on BLE |
| IMU | accel/gyro/mag (unused in the paper) | none in v1 (cut after review) | Meta did not use it; saves 4 parts |
| Power | battery, >4 h | protected LiPo 402030 (~200 mAh), charged off the band | no charger/USB on the body = no mains path to skin |

## 2. Architecture

```
 skin ── 48 dry pads ── electrode flex (passive, 2-layer PI, GND shield) ── 40-pin FPC
                                                                              │
   main board  ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
               ├─ 32× 4.7 kΩ (8 arrays) ─ ADS1298 U2 (ch 1–8, clock + bias master)
               │                         ADS1298 U3 (ch 9–16, clock from U2)
               │   bias: U2 RLD amp (1 MΩ‖1.5 nF) ─ 100 kΩ ─ middle pads of all pods
               ├─ SPI (2 CS, shared START/RESET/DRDY) ─ ESP32-S3-MINI-1 ─ Wi-Fi UDP
               ├─ 9 test pads: UART0, EN, BOOT, USB D+/D−, power (flashing, off-body only)
               └─ protected 1S LiPo (JST-SH) ─ AP2112K-3.3 (digital) + LP5907-3.0 (analog)
```

Schematics are generated by Python scripts (one connection list per board, reviewed as code):
`boards/neurband_main/gen_main_sch.py`, `boards/neurband_electrodes/gen_electrodes_sch.py`,
using `tools/schgen.py`. Run the script to regenerate the `.kicad_sch`; `tools/netcheck.py`
prints the netlist for checking.

## 3. Key decisions

1. **ADS1298 (not ADS1299).** Runs from a single 3.0 V analog rail (ADS1299 needs 5 V → boost
   converter), costs ~$20 instead of ~$62 at the fab, and at 4 kSPS / gain 6 its shorted-input noise
   (1.5 µVrms, 1048 Hz bandwidth, datasheet table 1) is below Meta's 2.46 µVrms. It integrates PGA,
   24-bit ADC, bias (right-leg) amplifier, lead-off detection and test signals: one IC per 8 channels.
2. **Passive electrodes, central AFE.** Meta has an amplifier in every pod. We run 10–15 cm of flex
   trace from each dry pad to the ADC. Risk: mains pickup and cable motion. Mitigations: pairs routed
   together, hatched GND shield on the outer flex layer, a driven bias contact in every pod, battery
   operation (no ground loop), 4.7 kΩ series resistors. If the bench shows too much pickup, iteration 2
   adds per-pod buffers (one dual op-amp per pod) — the flex pinout stays the same.
3. **DC-coupled inputs, gain 6, ±400 mV range.** Dry gold electrodes have large, drifting DC offsets;
   24 bits leave plenty of headroom so all filtering happens in software (20–850 Hz band-pass, notch).
   Watch for clipping when a contact lifts (lead-off detection reports it).
4. **Bias in every pod.** The middle pad of each pod is driven by U2's right-leg amplifier
   (1 MΩ ‖ 1.5 nF feedback, 100 kΩ series). Total bias contact area is large, so the body is held at
   mid-supply even if some pads lift. U3's RLD amp is off; its sense resistors join U2's via RLDINV
   (datasheet fig. 45).
5. **Two ADCs, one clock.** U2 runs on its internal oscillator and outputs it on CLK to U3; START is
   shared, so all 16 channels sample simultaneously. U2's DRDY is the only interrupt. RESET/PWDN are
   tied high; the firmware resets with the SPI RESET opcode (U2 first, then U3 once its clock runs).
   10 kΩ pull-ups on both CS and a pull-down on START keep the ADCs quiet while the ESP32 boots.
6. **ESP32-S3 + Wi-Fi.** 16 ch × 4 kSPS × 24 bit = 1.54 Mbit/s raw. That is easy over Wi-Fi UDP and
   beyond what BLE delivers in practice. The module brings its own antenna, flash and USB.
7. **Battery-only on the body (safety).** No USB connector, no charger: the board cannot be connected
   to a mains-powered PC while it touches skin. Flashing uses test pads off-body. Charge the protected
   cell externally. This also removed 10 parts.
8. **Two LDOs.** A separate low-noise 3.0 V LDO (LP5907, 6.5 µVrms) feeds only the ADC analog supply,
   isolated from Wi-Fi current bursts on the 3.3 V rail (AP2112K, 600 mA).

### Simplification pass (Codex Sol review, `review/sol_schematic_review.md`)

| Change | Parts saved | Done? |
|---|---|---|
| Drop IMU + I²C pull-ups | 4 | yes |
| Drop charger, charge LED, USB-C, ESD, CC resistors | 10 | yes |
| Drop status LED, battery divider, power switch | 6 | yes |
| Add CS pull-ups ×2, START pull-down | −3 | yes |
| Single LDO / AVDD from battery | 3 | no: noise risk |
| Drop input resistor arrays | 8 | no: they give RF/ESD/current limiting for ~0.36 µVrms |
| One ADS1298 (8 channels) | 16 | no: kept 16 channels to match Meta's data; U3 can be left unpopulated for an 8-channel build |

Result: **52 placed parts on the main board** (5 ICs/modules, 30 capacitors, 8 resistor arrays,
6 resistors, 2 connectors, 1 button) + 9 bare test pads. The electrode flex has no parts.

## 5. Safety

- Wear the band only when it runs from its battery and streams over Wi-Fi. Never connect a USB cable,
  debugger, charger or grounded scope while it touches skin. The series resistors are not mains isolation.
- Use a LiPo with a protection circuit (over-discharge/short).
- Bias current into the body is limited by 100 kΩ (≤ 30 µA even with the amplifier rail-to-rail on a
  3 V supply); input leads by 4.7 kΩ each.

### Open issues / next iteration
1. **Bench validation first:** shorted-input noise, mains pickup through the passive flex, dry-contact clipping
   (DC coupled, ±400 mV), bias-loop stability with real skin. These decide whether v2 needs per-pod buffers.
2. **Flex fold / pin-1 continuity:** confirm on the first assembled sample (`docs/layout/electrode_flex_layout.md`).
3. **Move J2 to a 34 mm short edge (board rev 2).** With J2 on the long edge the 54 mm board must run
   across the wrist and the module overhangs the band by 0.9 mm (XL) to 11.6 mm (S). On a short edge the
   module shrinks to ~40 mm across.
4. **Board length:** 54 mm (+5 mm antenna overhang) vs. the 48 mm target. Options: nFBGA ADS1298 or putting
   the resistor arrays on the bottom (adds a second assembly side).
5. **Strap pressure** varies ~2:1 around the wrist; Meta used spring pins. Cheap fix to try: foam under each
   pod shell, or soldered gold spring contacts on the pads.
6. **VCAP1 22 µF X5R is microphonic** (TI recommends a non-ferroelectric part under vibration): try a
   tantalum/polymer part if motion artefacts show up at the reference.
7. Compile and bring up the firmware (`docs/firmware.md` checklist).
